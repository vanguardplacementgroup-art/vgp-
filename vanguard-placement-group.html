Home → How It Works

How it works

Four steps between a file on your laptop and a named person at an agency watching it. No portal, no algorithm, no silence.

1

You send material

A reel, a self-tape, a scene, a resume — a link is enough. Messy is fine. We have shortlisted people off a single phone-shot scene with decent sound.

2

The desk watches everything

All of it, not the first ten seconds. We note what you are castable for right now, which scene proves it, and what is missing if anything is.

3

We match you to live briefs

Agency and casting desks tell us what they are building this season. Your material goes to the ones genuinely looking for what you do — by name, not by mass email.

4

You hear back either way

Shortlisted: we tell you which desks and when. Not yet: you get the notes, free, and an open door to come back once you have shot what is missing.

What a desk sees

The file we send

Every shortlist goes out in the same format. Clean, short, impossible to ignore.

01

One page, no padding

Name, playing age, location, union status, the single line that says what you are right for.

02

Timecoded scene

We tell the desk exactly where to start watching. Nobody scrubs for the good bit.

03

Why we are sending you

Two sentences on why you fit the brief they gave us. Specific, not flattering.

04

A way to say yes fast

Availability, contact, and a reply-to that reaches a person in minutes.

The Review Department

Vanguard Placement Group exists for one reason: independent talent with good material keeps getting lost in piles nobody finishes watching.

Who we are

A desk, not a directory

We are a small talent acquisitions team. People bring us reels and resumes, we watch everything properly, and the material that is ready goes to agents and casting directors we actually speak to.

There is no directory to pay into and no profile that sits there collecting dust. A shortlist is a real email, to a real person, with your scene timecoded in it.

When someone is not ready, we say that too — with the reasons. It is the part of the job people thank us for most.
